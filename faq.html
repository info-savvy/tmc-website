Everything you need to know before you travel to Madagascar — from visas and vaccinations to mora mora and Malagasy phrases.

“The more prepared you are, the more freely you can lose yourself in the experience.”

Madagascar rewards curious, open-minded travellers. This page brings together the most important practical information and our best on-the-ground tips — gathered over 16 years of guiding guests through the island.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Before You Go

Essentials
Do I need a visa?▼
Most travellers can obtain a tourist visa on arrival for stays of up to 90 days. Your passport should be valid for at least six months beyond your travel dates with at least one blank page. We recommend checking the latest requirements with your nearest Malagasy embassy before travelling.
What vaccinations are required?▼
A yellow fever certificate is required if arriving from or transiting through a country where yellow fever is present. Recommended: Hepatitis A & B, Typhoid, Tetanus and Rabies for extended rural travel. Consult your doctor or travel clinic before departure.
Is malaria a concern?▼
Malaria exists in some parts of Madagascar, particularly in coastal and lowland regions. Speak to your doctor about anti-malaria medication. Use insect repellent, wear long sleeves in evenings and sleep under mosquito nets where provided.
What is the best time to visit?▼
The dry season from April to November is generally best. May to October offers cooler temperatures and excellent wildlife viewing. September to November is ideal for whale sharks. December to March is warmer and greener but is the cyclone and rainy season in some regions.
What should I pack?▼
Lightweight breathable clothing, walking shoes, a light rain jacket, hat, sunscreen, insect repellent, swimwear, reusable water bottle, small medical kit, power bank, camera or binoculars and a waterproof bag. Warmer layers are useful in the highlands during June to August.
Is travel insurance recommended?▼
Yes — strongly recommended. Cover should ideally include medical emergencies, trip cancellations, emergency evacuation and adventure activities. Medical facilities outside major cities can be limited.

Money & Practicalities

On the Ground
What currency is used?▼
The Malagasy Ariary (MGA). Cash is widely used, especially outside cities. ATMs are available in larger towns but may not always be reliable — carry backup cash. Euros are the easiest foreign currency to exchange.
Are credit cards accepted?▼
At some larger hotels and restaurants in tourist areas. Many businesses, markets and rural establishments are cash-only. Visa is more widely accepted than Mastercard.
Can I drink tap water?▼
No. Drink sealed bottled or filtered water only. Avoid ice unless purified. Most hotels and lodges provide safe drinking water for guests.
Is Wi-Fi available?▼
In most hotels, lodges and cafés in major towns. Speeds vary. Local SIM cards with affordable data are available on arrival. Mobile coverage is generally good in populated areas.

Getting Around

Transport
How reliable is transportation?▼
Domestic flights may occasionally experience delays. Road travel can be slow in the rainy season. Boat transfers depend on tides and conditions. Madagascar is known for its mora mora lifestyle — slowly, slowly. We design itineraries with comfortable travel times built in.
Is self-driving recommended?▼
Not for first-time visitors. Roads can be challenging due to potholes, limited signage and seasonal flooding. Night driving is discouraged. Most visitors travel with an experienced local driver and guide.
How many days should I spend?▼
We recommend a minimum of 10–14 days. Travel distances are long, so many visitors focus on one or two regions. Three-week itineraries allow for much deeper exploration.

Safety & Health

Wellbeing
Is Madagascar safe for tourists?▼
Yes. Madagascar is considered a safe and welcoming destination, particularly in national parks, beach areas and popular travel regions. Use trusted guides and transport, avoid displaying valuables and avoid walking alone at night in cities.
What food can I expect?▼
Malagasy cuisine combines African, Asian and French influences. Expect fresh seafood, grilled meats, rice with vegetables and sauces, tropical fruits and vanilla desserts. Vegetarian options are available in most tourist areas.
Is it family-friendly?▼
Absolutely. Madagascar is excellent for families — children love lemurs, snorkelling, beach activities and forest walks. We tailor itineraries with suitable accommodation and travel times for all ages.
Can I see lemurs in the wild?▼
Madagascar is the only place on earth where lemurs exist naturally in the wild. Many national parks and reserves offer excellent close-range encounters with multiple species.

Culture & Language

Culture
What languages are spoken?▼
Malagasy and French are the official languages. Malagasy is the everyday language for most communities. French is widely used in hotels and tourism. English is spoken in tourist areas and by many guides.
Are there cultural etiquette rules?▼
Dress modestly in villages and sacred sites. Ask permission before photographing people. Respect local fady (taboos). Greet people politely before starting conversations. Malagasy people are warm and appreciate genuine respect.
What makes Madagascar unique?▼
Approximately 90% of Madagascar’s wildlife is found nowhere else on earth — including lemurs, chameleons, baobabs and rare birds and reptiles. Spectacular biodiversity, rich culture, stunning beaches and dramatic landscapes combine unlike anywhere else.
