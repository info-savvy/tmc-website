On The Ground Travel Tips

Travel Tips The Madagascar Collection 8 min read

Madagascar rewards curious, open-minded travellers. These tips come from 16 years of living and working on the island — they’re the things we tell every guest before they travel. If you have a question not covered here, we’re always happy to help directly.

“Small choices make a profound difference when you travel with intention.”

Embrace the Mora Mora Lifestyle

In Madagascar, life moves at a slower pace. Mora mora means “slowly, slowly” — a relaxed, patient approach woven into the culture. Things may not always run on schedule, but this slower rhythm is part of the island’s charm. Stay flexible and enjoy the experience as it unfolds.

  • Allow buffer time on all travel days
  • Pack your patience as carefully as your sunscreen
  • Embrace the unexpected — the best moments are often unplanned

Respect Local Culture & Traditions

Malagasy people are known for their warmth, hospitality and strong sense of community. Greet people before conversations, ask permission before photographing, dress respectfully in villages and be mindful of local fady (taboos).

  • Learn a few Malagasy words — greetings are warmly received
  • Dress modestly in villages and religious sites
  • Always ask before photographing people

Wildlife viewing in Madagascar

Travel Responsibly

Madagascar is one of the world’s most extraordinary biodiversity hotspots. Small choices — reef-safe sunscreen, reusable water bottles, supporting locally owned businesses — add up to make a real difference in preserving these fragile ecosystems.

  • Use reef-safe sunscreen when snorkelling
  • Never remove shells, coral or wildlife from natural areas
  • Stay on designated paths in parks and reserves
  • Support locally owned lodges, guides and artisans

Wildlife Viewing Tips

Early mornings and evenings offer the best wildlife activity. Many species are remarkably camouflaged — your guides are expert at spotting what you’d walk straight past. Bring binoculars for birds and a headlamp for night walks.

  • Never feed or touch wild animals
  • Observe quietly and respectfully
  • Early mornings offer the best sightings
  • Night walks reveal a completely different world

Marine life along the Madagascar coast

Ocean & Marine Activities

Madagascar’s coastline offers exceptional snorkelling, diving, whale watching and marine encounters. The reefs are extraordinary but fragile. Follow your guide’s instructions, never stand on coral, and choose environmentally friendly sunscreen.

  • Never touch or stand on coral reefs
  • Follow guide instructions during excursions
  • Use environmentally friendly sunscreen
  • Do not touch marine animals

Photography Tips

Madagascar is extraordinarily photogenic — from ancient baobabs to colourful markets and unique wildlife. Sunrise and sunset at the Avenue of the Baobabs is unmissable. Carry spare batteries, dust protection on road trips, and always ask before photographing people.

  • Sunrise and sunset at the Baobabs is unmissable
  • Dust protection essential on overland trips
  • Ask permission before photographing people
  • Binoculars double as excellent wildlife spotting tools
